# Learn copy tone

Write like a sharp coach talking to a competent adult athlete.
Do not write like a methods paper, product spec, or internal programming memo.

Before drafting or revising any Learn article, read the openings of two published articles in `content/articles/` and match that register.

## Voice

- Plain, direct, concrete
- Second person is fine ("you")
- Prefer real scenes: sore shoulder, Saturday match, hotel gym, missed Monday
- Keep sentences mostly short; vary rhythm
- Sound confident without sounding academic or corporate

## Core question

Every article should answer:

> What should the training plan do next?

That idea can stay. The wording around it must stay human.

## Prefer / avoid

| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| sore shoulder, angry knee | local limitation, constrained region |
| swap the lift | substitute by training intent |
| keep training legs and back | continue developing unconstrained qualities |
| what that exercise was there to do | training intent / slot function |
| how hard it felt the next day | tolerance evidence |
| change how the move loads you | alter the stress profile |
| one body part is bothering you | local limit / unit of analysis |
| keep the rest of the plan moving | preserve progressive stimulus elsewhere |

Avoid these patterns unless quoting another source:

- "unit of analysis"
- "local limit" / "local limitation"
- "constrained slot"
- "intent-matched"
- "stress profile"
- "programming constraint" as reader-facing phrasing
- "tolerance evidence"
- "stimulus distribution" without a plain-English translation beside it
- stacks of abstract nouns
- repeated "not X, but Y" constructions
- motivational slogans
- fake precision ("exactly 48 hours", universal percentages)

## Evidence voice

- Label evidence and judgment separately
- Cite carefully; do not overclaim
- Stay on the training side of the medical line
- Soft product close only: "IOFitness is being designed..."
- No diagnosis, rehab-protocol, or treatment claims

## Structure voice

Good article shape still looks like:

1. Human problem in the first screen
2. Short callout with the plain answer
3. A few clear decisions
4. One worked example with branches
5. Bottom line in ordinary language

Headings should sound spoken:

- Good: "Keep training the rest of your body"
- Bad: "Maintain progressive stimulus in unconstrained regions"

## Quick self-check

Before calling Learn copy done, answer yes to all:

1. Could a smart non-coach friend understand paragraph one cold?
2. Did I open with a person/situation, not a taxonomy?
3. Would I say this out loud to a client without cringing?
4. Did I remove internal/product jargon from reader-facing lines?
5. Does the piece still match the tone of articles 1–6?

If any answer is no, rewrite before shipping.
